## The core design philosophy — why Codolio looks generic

Codolio is a personal tracker. It shows your numbers in a clean card layout. It's basically a portfolio page. The problem: it has no _social context_, no _urgency_, no sense that you're competing with real people around you. It feels like a static resume page.

Your platform needs to feel like a **live arena** — something between GitHub's contribution graph, a leaderboard game, and a Bloomberg terminal for coding. Dark-first, data-dense but not cluttered, always showing you where you stand relative to others.

The three design pillars that separate you:

**1. University context is always visible** — your rank within LPU is always in the corner. Not a generic global rank — your actual batch rank. That's sticky.

**2. Motion signals liveness** — when someone's rank changes, it animates. The C-Score isn't a static number, it pulses when it updates.

**3. Identity over stats** — Codolio leads with numbers. You lead with the person: name, photo, university batch, then the numbers beneath.

## What separates this from Codolio — specifically

**1. "You" is always visible in the leaderboard.** Codolio shows your numbers in isolation. Your platform always surfaces _where you stand among your peers_ — your row is highlighted in purple wherever it falls in the table. That's the psychological hook. You don't just see your score, you see the two people above and below you, which creates competitive pressure to improve.

**2. The podium.** No coding platform has a top-3 podium with a gold highlight. It feels like a sports leaderboard, not a data dashboard. Students screenshot this and share it.

**3. Rank with context.** `#41 of 680 CSE students` is 10x more meaningful than a raw score. Codolio gives you your LeetCode rating. You give students their actual position in their university cohort.

**4. Achievements unlock over time.** The locked grey achievements (Top 5%, 500-day streak, University #1) give students something to chase. Codolio has no progression system — it's just a static tracker.

**5. The public profile URL** (`campusrank.dev/navdeep`) becomes something students put on their resume and LinkedIn instead of six separate platform links. This is the single biggest differentiation — it's a credible, verified URL that a recruiter can click and immediately understand the candidate.

**6. University-first identity.** Every screen shows LPU · CSE · Batch 2025. Codolio is generic. You're contextual — a student's rank means something because it's within a known institution and cohort.

## The design rules to stick to when building

These decisions are non-negotiable for the product to feel premium:

- **Purple (#534AB7) is your brand accent** — used only for C-Score, "you" highlights, and primary actions. Never overused.
- **Dark sidebar, light content area** — creates visual hierarchy, not a flat page.
- **0.5px borders everywhere** — thinner than typical, feels more refined.
- **Your rank row is always highlighted in purple-tint** — the user always knows where they are.
- **Rank deltas (↑8, ↓2)** on every leaderboard row — makes it feel alive, not static.
- **Font: system sans-serif + monospace for scores** — scores and ratings look better in mono, names and labels in sans.
- **No gradients on cards** — flat, clean, no decoration that competes with the data.
